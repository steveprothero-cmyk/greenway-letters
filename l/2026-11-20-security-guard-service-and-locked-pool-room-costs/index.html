Dear Committee Members
Security guard service and locked pool room: costs and a post-implementation report
1. Purpose
I have read the notice to residents headed “Pool Room and Bathroom Permanently Locked and Implementation of Security Guard Services”. The decision has been made, and I do not ask the Committee to revisit it. I write to ask that, at the end of the summer, the Committee report to all owners what the arrangement cost in full and what it achieved.
2. Why a report is needed
The 2026 AGM papers costed a summer guarding service at around $110,000 and set it aside as costly and impractical. The same papers record that the earlier measures — authorising Committee members to instruct trespassers to leave, discussions with Tuggeranong Police, and a protocol for action — were not effective, and that police were on most occasions unable to attend. The present arrangement is the Committee’s next attempt. The notice states no cost, no limit on how long a guard stays once called (“until the situation is resolved”), and no budget line. Owners cannot judge whether it was worth it without those figures and some measure of the result.
On 17 March 2026 I reported to the Committee the advice I received at Tuggeranong Police Station: that an authorised person should ask people to leave the pool area and, if they refuse, call police, and that people should not approach or engage. I ask whether that advice was taken into account in this decision, and whether the arrangement is, or will be, set out in a written protocol.
3. What I ask
(a) A report. By 30 April 2027 (the Pool Room rules treat November to March as the summer period), a written report circulated in full to all owners in the same way as the November notice, and before the notice for the 2027 annual general meeting issues. It should cover:
• full cost: every Leader Security invoice with date, time, duration, rate and amount; any retainer or standing charge; locks, signage and other related costs; any Grady Strata charge attributable to the arrangement; and the total, set against the $110,000 figure;
• attendances: how many, on what dates, how long each lasted, and how often police also attended (with event numbers);
• outcomes: how often people left when directed, any injuries or incidents, and how incidents compare with the same period last year;
• charges to residents: any guard cost charged to a resident for a guest’s behaviour, the amount and the basis;
• effect on residents: how many requests for access to the locked rooms were made by text, how quickly they were met, and any complaints received; and
• the fund the money came from, and the Committee’s assessment of whether to continue, vary or end the arrangement.
(b) Records from now. So that the report is written from facts, I ask that an incident register be kept from 20 November 2026 recording date, time, description, who called, guard arrival time, time on site, police event number, outcome and cost, and that the Committee record in its minutes what result would count as success.
4. Questions I ask now
1. What are the terms of the engagement with Leader Security: hourly rate, minimum call-out charge, any retainer, any cap on time on site, and insurance?
2. From which fund is the cost being paid, and which limb of section 77 of the Unit Titles (Management) Act 2011 (ACT) is relied on: approval in the general fund budget, or an ordinary resolution?
3. On what provision of the Act or the Owners Corporation Rules will a guard’s attendance be charged to a resident for a guest’s behaviour, how will responsibility be decided, and what review is available?
4. The notice describes the locking of the Pool Room and bathroom as both “permanent” and “temporary”. How long will it last, and how does it relate to the published Pool Room hours of 8:00am to 9:30pm from November to March?
If any answer is held in a record, I ask that this be treated as a request under section 120A, and that I be told the amount of any fee before it is incurred. Section 120A(2) requires access to be allowed within 14 days after the request is received.
5. Basis
Section 77 allows payments from the general fund only if they are approved in the budget or authorised by ordinary resolution. Section 35(3)(a) provides that the Committee must exercise its functions as the corporation directs by resolution at a general meeting. The Act does not oblige a committee to prepare a report. I would prefer that the Committee agree to this voluntarily, but I note that the route of a resolution exists.
I would be grateful for a reply by Friday 4 December 2026 confirming whether the Committee will provide the report, and answering the questions in section 4.
Yours sincerely
Stephen W Prothero
Owner, Unit 151
